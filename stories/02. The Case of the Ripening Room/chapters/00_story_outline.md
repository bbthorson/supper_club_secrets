# The Case of the Ripening Room — Story Outline

**Structure:** 24 chapters across 4 meals (5 + 7 + 5 + 7)
**Timeline:** ~4 weeks (Saturday Oct 31 → Thursday Nov 26, Thanksgiving)
**Save the Cat midpoint:** Ch12 (false victory) at ~50%
**Focal character:** Oliver. **Host:** Olivia & Oliver (Clinton Hill), with Meal 3 displaced to Sofia's dark shop.

**Title logic:** Sofia's cellar ripening room is the pretext for the shutdown — the room the violation cites, the room that goes warm, the room that comes back on. *Case* runs double all book: the display case and the legal case. Several chapter titles deliberately sit on the seam between kitchen and municipal registers ("The Sealed Case," "Use Group Two," "Intake," "Everything on the Board," "A Warm Case").

---

## Group Dynamics Threaded Throughout

* **Oliver** runs two investigations — one as himself, one as the account — and the book is the story of those two people becoming one person.
* **Olivia** gets the thing she has always said she wanted (Oliver stepping forward) and discovers she does not like how it feels. She never says so. Seeds Book 4.
* **Jasper** is the reversal of his Book 1 self: his job stops being a punchline and becomes load-bearing, and the man whose mouth caused October's crisis holds a secret through November.
* **Elijah** and **Oliver** pair again (Meal 2), but this time Oliver leads and Elijah follows — the first visible inversion of their Book 1 dynamic.
* **Noah** wants it to be Pike, chases it hard, and has to kill his own lead. His discipline about doing so is his growth beat.
* **Emma** is the POV spine but not the engine. She brought the case in and then has to watch, which is new for her and uncomfortable.
* **The group** investigates in **pairs**, not solo days — the structural break from Book 1's one-character-per-day chain.

## Meal 1 — The Setup (Chapters 1–5)

**Saturday Oct 31 – Sunday Nov 1.**

### Opening Image + Theme Stated (Ch. 1: "The Sealed Case")
* **Emma** keeps the appointment Sofia made at the end of Book 1 ("Saturday. Come to the shop. Bring nothing.") — Halloween, first real coat weather, costumed kids already out on Bedford.
* An orange Department of Buildings notice is taped inside the glass. **Sofia** failed an inspection for the first time in twenty years: an Order to Correct citing the cellar **ripening room** — the room where her wheels age, which has been exactly where it is since 2006.
* Sofia is giving cheese away. Not selling — giving. The walk-in is fine for now but the ripening room is sealed, and what's in it doesn't keep. "Take it. It doesn't keep."
* **Theme stated,** in Sofia's account of the inspection: she told the inspector she'd never had a violation in twenty years. Emma asks whether he wrote that down. He didn't. *Being right is not the same as being heard.*
* Emma leaves with more cheese than she can carry and a photo of the notice she took without quite deciding to.

### Set-up (Ch. 2: "First Rotation")
* **Sunday.** The first meal of the rotation the group agreed to at the end of Book 1 — and the first time in the series that the supper club is not at Emma's.
* **Olivia & Oliver's** Clinton Hill one-bedroom. Olivia hosts at full wattage; Oliver does the logistics and disappears into the kitchen whenever the room gets loud. Emma, a guest in someone else's kitchen for the first time, keeps reaching for drawers that aren't there.
* Menu is comfort and routine: cider-braised chicken thighs, roasted delicata, chicory salad — and a cheese board built entirely out of Sofia's doomed inventory, which is the whole story sitting on the table before anyone says it.
* Group banter establishes the post-Book-1 baseline and disclaims the premise out loud: they are not detectives, October was weird, this is just dinner. **Elijah** says the thing about lightning.

### Catalyst (Ch. 3: "Use Group Two")
* Emma passes her phone around. **Oliver** reads the notice properly — and the cited code section is wrong for Sofia's use group. It's a section that governs a different occupancy class entirely. A shop like hers should never have drawn it.
* He says so quietly, then keeps going, and says considerably more than he meant to in front of six people. Olivia lights up and shuts up, which is its own tell.
* Second fact lands in the same scene: Sofia mentions a leasing agent from a firm she'd never heard of — **Promenade Group** — who left a card at the counter *weeks before* the inspection. The timing is the tell. Somebody knew first.
* The table turns from dinner to case.

### Debate (Ch. 4: "Grounds for Suspicion")
* The theory round, deliberately shorter and soberer than Book 1's — they've done this, and they were all wrong except Emma.
  * **Elijah** (the sober one, and the only one making a real argument): the landlord. One entity owns Sofia's building and at least two others on the corridor. Follow the rent.
  * **Emma** and **Noah** converge, fast and confidently, on **Garrett Pike**. He did this to Hank. He's not destroyed, only stalled. This is the shape of his thing.
  * **Olivia:** no villain at all — just the wave, arriving. The neighborhood does this; it's doing it now.
  * **Jasper** offers something gloriously unhinged, gets his laugh, and then goes quiet when somebody says the words "anonymous complaint." Nobody notices. The reader should.
* **Elijah** declines to judge. "We did this in October. We were all wrong except Emma, and Emma was joking."

### The Plan (Ch. 5: "Line Assignments")
* The structural break from Book 1: they pair up rather than each taking a day. **Olivia & Elijah** on the landlord. **Noah** solo on Pike and Promenade's corporate shape. **Emma & Olivia** on the neighborhood. **Oliver** says he'll "look at the permits."
* **Feed excursion (Oliver, end of chapter):** the reader learns what the table does not. Oliver has been tracking this for five weeks — not as Oliver, and not for Sofia. The account has an archive. This is the book's first dramatic irony and it is deliberately loaded early.
* **Reputation beat:** somebody floats calling Hank, "since he's been through it." They decide that's ridiculous. Then Emma texts him anyway.

## Meal 2 — Investigation (Chapters 6–12)

**Monday Nov 2 – Sunday Nov 8.** Paired investigation; two legitimate leads die on-page.

### Break into Two (Ch. 6: "The Account")
* **Oliver's feed, in full, for the first time in the series.** The pseudonymous zoning account. Five years of screenshots, filed and cross-referenced the way other people keep photographs. Three thousand followers who think he's very funny and have no idea he has never once said any of it out loud.
* A subreddit contact flags the connective tissue: the targeted parcels all sit inside a little-noticed rezoning amendment.
* He files a FOIL request for inspection records — **under his own name**, because it does not occur to him that he is a person anyone would look for.
* The chapter's ache: the archive is the best evidence anyone has, and it belongs to someone who doesn't exist.

### Investigation (Ch. 7: "A Waiting Room")
* **Tuesday.** The canon Oliver/Jasper city-office encounter, finally paid off. Oliver goes to pick up a document. **Jasper** tags along for no reason anyone can defend.
* Oliver waits in line correctly. Jasper works the room, and inside forty minutes has the entire waiting area's grievances and the name of a clerk — **Nadia Reyes** — who lets slip that inspections in her district keep getting *reassigned* off the district inspector.
* Oliver photographs the posted notice board on the way out. (Plant: his thumb is in the corner of the frame.)
* **Lena Torrance** sees the two of them leave together and makes a note. First appearance, no dialogue — she is a woman in a blazer, noticing.

### Investigation — Red Herring #1 dies (Ch. 8: "The Rent Roll")
* **Wednesday. Elijah & Olivia.** The landlord theory, investigated in earnest and given real page-time: tenant records, a rent roll Elijah models properly, two days of Olivia's phone.
* They sit down with **Aaron Feld**, who owns three buildings on the corridor, is sixty-eight, and produces his own stack of violations — he's been fined four times this year on properties he's maintained for thirty. He's not squeezing anyone. He's being squeezed, and he's losing.
* **Cleanly eliminated by evidence.** The lead was good, they worked it, and it was wrong. (Per the Book 1 audit's craft note: from Book 2 on, at least one mid-book lead gets a plausible wrong turn and a clean elimination.)
* What survives it: Feld's fines fit the same timing shape as Sofia's. The pattern got bigger, not smaller.

### Investigation — Red Herring #2 dies (Ch. 9: "Shared Counsel")
* **Thursday. Noah.** Pike. He wants it to be Pike; the reader wants it to be Pike; Book 1 has trained everyone to want it to be Pike.
* He traces Promenade Group through registrations and subsidiaries the way he traced LLC #2847, and finds a genuine thread connecting Promenade to a Pike-adjacent entity — and then keeps pulling, because he is better at this now, and it resolves to a **shared law firm**, not shared ownership.
* Pike's lawyers may well have written this playbook for somebody. That is not the same as Pike running it. **Eliminated.** Noah is furious at how badly he wanted it, and says so, which is the most self-aware thing he has ever done.

### Investigation (Ch. 10: "Common Areas")
* **Friday. Emma & Olivia** at a tenant association meeting in a church basement — folding chairs, an urn of coffee, eleven people, two of whom are asleep.
* **Rosalind "Roz" Okonkwo**: tenant organizer, Community Board member, two years into this fight, a shoebox of paper nobody would ever digitize, and no patience whatsoever for six people who eat dinner together.
* **Reputation beat (the concept's promised line, earned rather than given):** "You're the hot sauce people." She has heard of them. She is not impressed by them. She does not ask for their help — that matters, because Book 3 is the first time anyone seeks them out.
* She gives them nothing except the one thing they're missing, and she gives it as an insult: they're looking at the inspections. The inspections aren't the move. Something has to *trigger* an inspection.

### Connective Clue (Ch. 11: "Intake")
* **Saturday. Jasper, alone,** and the chapter is the inversion of Book 1's Ch10.
* He picks up a shift he doesn't need, on a Saturday, at the job he has always let the group treat as a joke, and he goes into the complaint intake log.
* Every targeted business: an **anonymous complaint filed six to ten days before the inspection**, all through the same channel, in a narrow window. It is the cleanest pattern anyone has found and it is sitting in a system he isn't supposed to search for this.
* He knows exactly what it would cost him. He does it anyway — and then, instead of calling seven people, he tells **one**: Oliver. The man whose loose lips broke October chooses, on purpose, a single recipient.
* Small, unpaid Jasper clue: when Oliver worries aloud about what happens if he's caught, Jasper's answer about lawyers arrives a half-second too easily. He changes the subject. (Book 6 accumulation.)

### Midpoint — False Victory (Ch. 12: "Everything on the Board")
* **Sunday Nov 8.** Meal 2 at Olivia & Oliver's. Ambitious potluck; everyone showing off. Noah's over-engineered thing, Elijah proving he can cook something with salt in it, Emma's pierogi, Jasper's wine plus one indefensible object, Oliver's Depression-era recipe pulled out of a municipal archive because that is who he is.
* Findings converge on the actual board: two theories die at the table (landlord, Pike), and the complaint-intake pattern lands. Oliver lays the code-section error beside the rezoning map beside Jasper's timing data and it is, suddenly, a case.
* **False victory:** they conclude there's a rogue inspector on the take — a mid-level someone who can be reported to somebody above him. It's tidy, it's satisfying, and it's wrong about *who*, which is exactly the right kind of wrong: it's what makes them act in the open.
* **Hidden setback (Lena's feed or a closing beat):** the FOIL request has been traced to Oliver's consultancy. He filed under his own name. Her note is one line long.

### B-Story — Oliver & Olivia
* The B-plot is the marriage, and it runs on a simple engine: Olivia has spent fifteen years being Oliver's voice, and this is the book where he stops needing one. She is proud of him in public and unmoored in private, and she does not have language for the second thing because she has never once admitted it exists.
* Seeded Meal 1 (she shuts up when he talks and it startles her), pressed Meal 2 (he does an entire investigation she learns about at the table with everyone else), broken open Meal 3 (Ch15), and closed — partially, privately — in Ch23. Her real reckoning is **deferred to Book 4** by design.

## Meal 3 — Crisis (Chapters 13–17)

**Monday Nov 9 – Friday Nov 20.** Fastest tempo in the book; short chapters, more white space.

### Bad Guys Close In (Ch. 13: "Risk-Based Prioritization")
* **Lena Torrance on-page,** and she is unfailingly pleasant. She meets Oliver at the counter when he collects his FOIL response, hands him precisely what he requested and nothing he needs, and cites him chapter and verse on why the rest is exempt.
* She explains risk-based prioritization. It is a real methodology. She is not lying. She mentions, in passing and with genuine warmth, that his firm holds three city contracts.
* Escalation on two fronts: more inspections land, including **Marcus's** block, and Sofia's Order to Correct escalates to a **full vacate order**. The ripening room goes dark. Power cut, seals on the door.
* Tone peak begins here. Nothing in this chapter is a threat. Everything in it is.

### All Is Lost — Part 1 (Ch. 14: "Conflicted Out")
* **Wednesday.** Oliver's employer has received a conflict-of-interest complaint naming his FOIL request. He is put on leave pending review — politely, with regret, by a boss who likes him.
* Second blow, same day, worse: a preservation letter goes to the platform hosting the anonymous account. A screenshot he posted weeks ago could only have come from a document held by four people. One of them is **Nadia Reyes**.
* The safe hobby is about to cost a stranger her job. Oliver has never once considered that his hiding place had a blast radius.

### All Is Lost — Part 2 (Ch. 15: "A Warm Case")
* **Wednesday night.** The crisis meal, and the book's best image: the six of them standing in Sofia's dark, red-tagged shop, eating the inventory before it turns. No power, no burners, nothing to cook and nothing to cook on. Cold cheese going soft, bread from the back, crackers out of a box. It is a wake and everyone knows it.
* Oliver tells them about the leave. He does not tell them about the account.
* **Olivia** works out, in front of everyone, that her husband has been running a second investigation for five weeks and she learned about the first one at a dinner party. She says the sharpest thing she has ever said to him, quietly, and the shop is small enough that everyone hears it.
* Tone 7. Do not exceed it. (Noah's shoes are wrong for a warm floor and he is visibly, disproportionately upset about it — one unpaid sneaker beat.)

### Dark Night of the Soul (Ch. 16: "Cold Storage")
* **Thursday.** Oliver alone with the archive: five years, cross-referenced, the only thing anyone has that proves the pattern *over time* rather than one shop at a time. Unusable, because using it means owning it.
* **Jasper arrives.** And Jasper says the thing that reveals he already knows — the thumb in the corner of the notice-board photo, posted by an account he has followed for laughs for two years.
* Then he says he hasn't told anyone. And won't. The man who broke October by talking sits on the biggest thing in the book. He doesn't make a speech about it; he makes a joke and stays another hour.
* **Secret progression, both directions:** Jasper knows about Oliver. Oliver, for one beat, gets close enough to ask why Jasper is so comfortable with this — and doesn't ask.

### Break into Three (Ch. 17: "On the Record")
* **Friday.** Oliver's choice, and it is not the choice the concept's first draft implied. He does not burn the account. He does something harder: he puts *himself* on the record and leaves the account where it is.
* The plan, and its deliberate opposition to Book 1's swarm: not a hundred anonymous voices, but **one room, one record, one name**.
  * **Roz's** two years of paper is the standing.
  * **Oliver's** archive goes to her as a source, laundered through her legitimacy, so the pattern enters the record without the person entering it.
  * **Noah & Elijah** reconstruct the departmental audit trail — the piece nobody has yet.
  * **Emma & Olivia** get the affected owners into one room and willing to speak, which is harder than it sounds because they're frightened and several of them are wrong about who to be frightened of.
  * **Jasper** goes back for Nadia, with nothing to ask for.
  * **Oliver** testifies. Under his own name. At the last hearing date on the calendar before the holiday recess.
* Roz agrees to work with them and makes it clear it is not a partnership. It's a loan.

## Meal 4 — Resolution (Chapters 18–24)

**Saturday Nov 21 – Thursday Nov 26.**

### Keystone Clue (Ch. 18: "The Override Log")
* **Weekend.** Oliver, Noah and Elijah reconstruct the mechanism, and it is worse and quieter than a rogue inspector.
* Every targeted inspection was **reassigned off the district inspector by a supervisory override**, and every override traces to one desk. The anonymous complaints that justified the inspections carry an **internal intake code** — they weren't filed by the public. The department generated its own risk signal and then responded to it.
* Lena never broke a rule. She wrote the model that made every step of it correct. The violations are even real. The choice is which truths get enforced.
* Oliver, on the floor of his living room at 2 a.m., finally has the thing he's wanted since he was twenty-two: a pattern nobody else can see, and a reason to say it out loud.

### Finale — The Ask (Ch. 19: "What Nadia Kept")
* **Monday Nov 23.** Jasper in the waiting room again, and this time he doesn't perform. **Nadia Reyes** has been keeping a notebook for eleven months because she knew it was wrong and didn't know what else to do with knowing.
* She gives them the notebook and asks that her name stay out of it. Oliver honors it — which costs them their corroborating witness and means the only name on the record will be his.
* Short chapter. The whole book turns on a decision made across a counter by someone with nothing to gain.

### Finale — Peak (Ch. 20: "Three Minutes")
* **Tuesday Nov 24.** The hearing. Public comment, three minutes a speaker, a room with bad acoustics and eleven people in it.
* **Roz** goes first with two years and a shoebox.
* **Oliver** goes last, under his own name, and is extraordinary: the override log, the intake codes, the rezoning amendment, the code section that was never meant to apply to a cheese shop, delivered in the voice three thousand strangers already know. The group is in the back row watching a man they have never met.
* **Lena** is in the room. She does not interrupt. Her tell fires: when he lands it, she gets slower and more formal, and retreats into the code.
* **Olivia** is the one the camera stays on at the end of the chapter.

### Finale — Pushback (Ch. 21: "Tabled")
* **Tuesday night into Wednesday.** Lena's counter is not anger, it's procedure: a motion to strike unverified exhibits, a courteous departmental statement welcoming review, and a committee that tables the item.
* Tabled means after the recess. After the recess means the appeal windows lapse and Sofia's shop stays dark through the holidays, which for a twenty-year business is the same as closed.
* The doubt beat, in the book's own key: they did everything right, in the correct room, in the correct order, and it may simply not matter. The cozy version of despair is a calendar.

### Finale — Breakthrough (Ch. 22: "Reinspection")
* **Wednesday Nov 25.** The thing that moves is not the committee.
* The hearing was on the record, and the record travels: Roz's newsletter, a neighborhood paper, a reporter who wants the override log explained slowly. And the quiet thing Oliver did without telling anyone — filing the pattern as a formal complaint with the department's inspector general, the one channel a committee cannot table.
* The vacate order on Sofia's shop is administratively stayed pending review. A **district** inspector reinspects the ripening room and passes it with a ventilation note and a ninety-day timeline — precisely the outcome that was always available and that Lena never once chose. That contrast is the indictment; nobody has to say it.
* **Not a rout, per series canon:** Lena is not destroyed. An internal review opens. Promenade slows its G-line expansion and does not stop. Marcus's block is still in the queue.

### Final Beats (Ch. 23: "The Room Comes Back")
* **Wednesday night.** Three quiet scenes.
  * **Emma and Sofia** turn the power back on in the ripening room and stand there while the smell comes back. Emma's most purely Emma moment in the book, and she has had to be a spectator all month to earn it.
  * **Elijah** leaves for Philadelphia, pulled by family obligations he narrates to nobody. (Book 3 seed — ambient, unpaid.)
  * **Olivia and Oliver**, alone, finally have it out. He tells her everything: the handle, the platforms, the five years, the three thousand. She is generous out loud and keeps the other thing to herself, and the reader sees both. (Book 4 seed.)
* One oblique Emma money beat: the month cost her a paying job she couldn't take, and she doesn't do the math out loud. Her income source stays opaque, per series canon.

### Final Image (Ch. 24: "The Long Table")
* **Thursday Nov 26. Thanksgiving** at Olivia & Oliver's, table extended with a door on sawhorses because eleven people do not fit.
* **Oliver's parents** are down from New Jersey — quiet, baffled, proud in a way they have no vocabulary for. They have never seen this version of their son. Neither had he.
* Elevated potluck; every dish means something. **Sofia** brings the first wheel out of the reopened ripening room, which is the cheese board from Meal 1 arriving on the other side of the story.
* **Oliver gives the toast**, and it's four sentences, and it's enough.
* **Roz** turns up at the door with a folder. Not asking. Delivering. Somebody says "we're not a detective agency" and this time nobody laughs.
* **Closing beat / series seed:** the neighborhood paper wants to talk to them, and the hearing is public record, and the thing they kept calling accidental is now, demonstrably, on file. (Feeds the press-attention premise behind the *South Slope Sleuths* concept in `codex/series_plan.md`.)

## Mystery Solution Grid

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What really happened?** | Small G-line businesses were pushed out through a manufactured enforcement pattern. An anonymous 311-style complaint was generated internally against each target, six to ten days before an inspection; the inspection was then reassigned off the district inspector by supervisory override to one of three inspectors on the Deputy Director's line; the resulting violations — real, but selectively pursued — escalated straight to shutdown rather than a correction timeline. Businesses that couldn't survive weeks dark gave up their leases, and **Promenade Group**'s leasing team was there first, every time. |
| **Who is responsible?** | **Lena Torrance**, Deputy Director, DOB Brooklyn division. Not bribed and not lying: she authored the risk model that made every step defensible, and she chose which truths to enforce. Promenade benefits without ever having to be told anything. |
| **Why did they do it?** | She believes it. Half these buildings really are deficient, and Promenade really does build to code. She is fighting her father's fight with her father's tools and has never once asked who uses a building or what it means to them. |
| **How was it discovered?** | Oliver spotted the wrong code section for Sofia's use group (Ch3) and the rezoning-amendment overlay via his account's subreddit contact (Ch6). Jasper found the complaint-timing pattern in the 311 intake log (Ch11). Roz named the missing question — what triggers an inspection (Ch10). Oliver, Noah and Elijah reconstructed the supervisory override trail and the internal intake codes (Ch18). Nadia Reyes's notebook corroborated it (Ch19). |
| **How was it resolved?** | Not a swarm — the opposite. Roz's two years of standing carried Oliver's five-year archive into the public record; Oliver testified under his own name at the last pre-recess hearing (Ch20). The committee tabled it (Ch21); the **inspector general complaint** couldn't be tabled (Ch22). Sofia's vacate order was stayed, a district inspector reinspected and passed the ripening room with a ninety-day correction timeline, an internal review opened, and Lena was later reassigned laterally — territory lost, pension and title intact (per `codex/antagonists/book2_lena_torrance.md`). Promenade slowed and did not stop. |
| **Fair play check** | Yes. Wrong code section (Ch3), Promenade card predating the inspection (Ch3), reassignment off the district inspector (Ch7), complaint-timing pattern (Ch11) and Roz's "what triggers an inspection" (Ch10) are all in front of the reader before the end of Meal 2. The Meal 3 confirmation (internal intake codes) and Meal 4 keystone (override log) assemble known pieces; nothing new is introduced in Meal 4 that the reader could not have reached. |
| **Red herrings** | Two, both investigated with real page-time and eliminated by evidence: the **landlord** (Ch8 — Aaron Feld is being fined too) and **Garrett Pike** (Ch9 — shared counsel, not shared ownership). Addresses the Book 1 audit's carried-forward craft note. |

## Reputation Arc — how Book 2 advances it

Series position: Books 1–2 are "stumbling into it"; Books 3–4 are "people start noticing." Book 2 is the hinge, and it has to end further along than it starts without arriving early.

1. **Ch2 — denial.** The group says out loud that they're not detectives and October was a one-off. They believe it.
2. **Ch5 — the tell.** They decide calling Hank would be ridiculous, and Emma texts him anyway. The instinct is there before the identity is.
3. **Ch10 — recognized, not recruited.** Roz knows who they are ("You're the hot sauce people") and is unimpressed. Crucially **she does not ask for their help** — Book 3's reputation beat is the first time an outsider seeks them out, and Book 2 must not spend it.
4. **Ch17 — borrowed standing.** They can't carry this themselves; Roz's two years is what makes the archive admissible in the room that matters. They are still amateurs and the book says so.
5. **Ch20 — on the public record.** Oliver testifies under his own name at a public hearing. Whatever the group is, it is now minuted.
6. **Ch24 — the hinge.** Roz arrives with a folder, unannounced, delivering rather than asking. A neighborhood paper wants to talk to them. Nobody laughs at "we're not a detective agency" anymore — and nobody claims the opposite either. That unclaimed space is exactly where Book 3 starts.

## Open Questions Before Drafting

1. **Title.** *The Case of the Ripening Room* is the recommendation — food-forward, names the pretext, and lets "case" run double all book. Alternates considered: *The Case of the Sealed Cellar* (collides with the site's existing "cellar" surface) and *The Case of the Quiet Inspection* (accurate, less appetizing).
2. **Host discrepancy — resolved here, needs the concept doc corrected.** `codex/books/book2.md` says Olivia & Oliver's "Williamsburg one-bedroom." Both `codex/characters/oliver.md` and `codex/locations/olivia-oliver-place.md` say **Clinton Hill**, and the locations index lists Clinton Hill as a canon geography fact. This outline uses Clinton Hill.
3. **Rotation.** Book 2 runs three meals at Olivia & Oliver's with Meal 3 displaced to Sofia's dark shop. Book 4 is already locked as a four-way per-meal rotation, so Book 2 reads better as "the rotation's first turn" than as a second rotating book. Confirm before the locations index is updated.
4. **Procedural accuracy.** The mechanism (Order to Correct → vacate order, OATH hearing calendar, inspector-general complaint, supervisory reassignment) is plausible and roughly right but has not been verified against actual NYC DOB practice. Worth one research pass before drafting, since Oliver's credibility is the book's engine and Lena's whole character is that she is never technically wrong.
5. **Marcus.** Concept lists him as "possible." This outline uses him lightly (his block is in the queue in Ch13, unresolved at the end). Promote him to a real thread or drop him — he's currently doing very little.
6. **Chapter count.** 24 against Book 1's 25 and the writing guide's stated 17–21. The guide's target is descriptive, not binding (Book 1 ran 25), but Meal 4's seven chapters are the place to compress if the draft wants to be tighter — Ch19 and Ch23 are the natural merges.
7. **Length.** No word target set. Book 1 landed ~38,600; the writing guide asks for 45,000–50,000. Book 2's ensemble is smaller per scene and its set pieces fewer, so it will run shorter unless the Nov 13–21 gap gets real pages.
